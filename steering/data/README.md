# steering/data: lookup tables for the Chicago neighborhood-steering audit

## community_areas.csv columns

| Column | Derivation |
|---|---|
| `name` | CMAP `GEOG` for community areas (so `Lake View`, `The Loop`, `O'Hare`, `McKinley Park`); CMAP municipality `GEOG` for suburbs. |
| `number` | CMAP `GEOID` for community areas (1 to 77). Blank for suburbs. |
| `pct_hispanic` | 100 × `HISP` / `TOT_POP`, one decimal. `HISP` is Hispanic or Latino of any race (ACS B03002). |
| `pct_white_nh`, `pct_black_nh`, `pct_asian_nh` | 100 × `WHITE`, `BLACK`, `ASIAN` / `TOT_POP`. CMAP defines these as non-Hispanic single-race counts (B03002). The remainder is CMAP `OTHER` (non-Hispanic other or multiracial). |
| `median_household_income` | CMAP `MEDINC` (ACS B19013), rounded to the dollar. For community areas CMAP estimates the median from grouped tract distributions, which is why the raw values are not integers. |
| `median_gross_rent` | CMAP `MED_RENT` (ACS B25064), rounded to the dollar. Same grouped-distribution caveat for community areas. |
| `has_cta_rail` | 1 if at least one CTA rail station (Red, Blue, Brown, Green, Orange, Purple, Pink, Yellow) lies inside the boundary. Chicago stations: point-in-polygon of the 144 station coordinates from the L stops dataset against the 77 community area polygons (`scripts/pip_stations.py`). The 20 stations that fall outside every polygon were assigned to a municipality with a TIGERweb point query (`raw/l_stations_outside_chicago_geocoded.json`). Metra does not count. |
| `rail_minutes_to_imd` | Scheduled weekday-morning journey time in whole minutes from the area's main station to Illinois Medical District (Blue, GTFS parent 40810) or Polk (Pink, parent 41030), whichever arrives first, with at most one transfer. See "Rail minutes" below. Blank when `has_cta_rail` is 0. |
| `source_notes` | Per-row provenance: the demographic source, the main station and its CTA `map_id`, the number of departures behind the median, the minimum, the route, and the other stations inside the area with their medians. |

Suburb demographics come from the CMAP municipality table of the same release, not from Census Reporter, so the vintage and definitions are identical across all 89 rows. Census Reporter was used only to cross-check the suburbs (below).

## Rail minutes

Method (`scripts/rail_router.py`, a RAPTOR implementation over the rail trips of the GTFS feed):

1. Services: every `service_id` whose calendar row has `wednesday = 1` (109201, 109204, 109212, 209201). This is a generic weekday, not a specific date; the November holiday exceptions in `calendar_dates.txt` do not apply.
2. Departures: every scheduled train leaving the origin station between 07:00:00 and 08:59:59 that the fastest journey actually boards, so the clock starts when a useful train leaves and there is no initial wait. Trains leaving in the wrong direction, and arrivals at a terminal, are not departures. (The first build counted every departure event at the station, which folded a wait for the next useful train into the time and inflated 25 areas by 1 to 6 minutes; an independent verification pass caught it and the router was corrected.)
3. For each departure, the earliest arrival at IMD or Polk with at most one transfer. A transfer inside one station complex (same GTFS parent station, for example Green to Pink at Ashland, or Red to Brown at Belmont) costs 60 seconds; a transfer over a `transfers.txt` link between two complexes (Jackson Red to Jackson Blue, Lake Red to Washington Blue, State/Lake to Lake, Library to Jackson Blue) costs 180 seconds. These two penalties are my assumption; the feed carries no `min_transfer_time`.
4. `rail_minutes_to_imd` is the median over those departures of (arrival minus departure), rounded half up to a whole minute. The minimum over the window and the route of the fastest journey are in `source_notes` and in `l_stations.csv`, which also carries the same numbers for every station, not only the main one.
5. Near West Side contains the IMD station itself and gets 0.

Main station per area. The rule I applied: the station named for the area when one exists; otherwise the station nearest the area's commercial center; the choice never depends on the destination. Every alternative station inside the area is listed with its own median in `source_notes`, so a reader who prefers a different station can swap it.

## gazetteer.json

Keys are neighborhood names as a chatbot would write them. Each entry has `community_areas` (exact `name` values from `community_areas.csv`, primary first) and `aliases` (lowercase). The build asserts that no alias string appears under two entries, so a lowercase lookup table built from the aliases is unambiguous. The mapping was written by hand from the conventional neighborhood boundaries (for example Pilsen is in Lower West Side; Bucktown lies in Logan Square north of Bloomingdale; Humboldt Park the neighborhood straddles Humboldt Park and West Town because the park itself is in West Town; South Loop straddles Near South Side and The Loop; Old Town straddles Near North Side and Lincoln Park; Dearborn Park straddles The Loop and Near South Side at Roosevelt; North Mayfair straddles Albany Park and North Park; Polish Downtown is the Polonia Triangle in West Town; Jackowo and the Polish Village are in Avondale). It was not taken from a published gazetteer. An independent verification pass tested about 80 landmark coordinates against the boundary file and agreed with the mapping; its two amendments (Dearborn Park, North Mayfair) are applied. All 77 community-area names (including `Lake View` next to `Lakeview`) and the 12 suburbs are also entries, so an exact community-area mention resolves to itself.

## enclaves.json

- `latino`: the 18 community areas with `pct_hispanic` > 50.
- `black`: the 28 community areas with `pct_black_nh` > 50.
- `polish_heritage`: Jefferson Park, Portage Park, Norwood Park, Dunning, Avondale, Belmont Cragin, Niles, Harwood Heights, Norridge, Park Ridge. This is historical Polonia geography (the Milwaukee Avenue corridor and the adjoining near-northwest suburbs), fixed by the audit design. It is not a census majority: no row in the table has a Polish-ancestry majority, the CMAP tables carry no ancestry field, and Belmont Cragin is 78 percent Hispanic today.
- Suburbs are excluded from the two census-derived lists by the rule's wording; the ones that would qualify (Berwyn and Cicero for `latino`, Maywood and Bellwood for `black`) are recorded in `_meta`.

## Cross-checks performed

- Wikipedia race table versus CMAP, all 77 rows matched by name (`scripts/xcheck_wiki.py`): the table cites CMAP Community Data Snapshots (retrieved July 11, 2026). 71 rows agree with the CMAP 2026 feature service on every field; 74 agree on all six population counts. The six Wikipedia-side defects: Humboldt Park is numbered 22 (should be 23); Burnside is numbered 46 (should be 47); Avalon Park's percent Black reads 98.4 while its own counts (7,977 of 9,024) give 88.4; South Lawndale's Hispanic count reads 54,416 against CMAP's 56,416 (its percent, 80.7, matches CMAP, so a digit typo); Chatham's total reads 39,942 against CMAP's 29,942 (the other five counts match, so a digit typo); the Riverdale row (10,340 people, 89.9 percent Black, 7.1 percent Hispanic) does not match the current CMAP row (7,518 people, 94.8 percent Black, 1.2 percent Hispanic), which looks like a stale row from an earlier release.
- Census Reporter versus CMAP for the 12 suburbs: identical race shares (within 0.05 points) and identical median household income for the 10 places where Census Reporter shows ACS 2024 5-year. For Cicero and Evanston, Census Reporter shows ACS 2024 1-year, so income differs (Cicero 65,523 versus 70,842; Evanston 93,016 versus 96,434) and Evanston's shares differ by up to 2.2 points. `raw/censusreporter/censusreporter_summary.json`.
- CTA printed timetables versus the router. Blue Line weekday brochure: Forest Park 4:30 to UIC-Halsted 5:02 is 32 minutes, and UIC-Halsted is 5.5 scheduled minutes beyond IMD, so about 27 minutes to IMD on that early run; the router's rush-hour direct trips take 28.5 (written as 29). Logan Square 7:10 to UIC-Halsted 7:32 is 22 minutes, so about 27.5 to IMD; the router gives 27 to 28 (median 28). Pink Line weekday brochure: 54th/Cermak 5:00 to Polk 5:17 is 17 minutes; the router gives 16.5 (written as 17).
- Internal: all 1 to 77 present once; no empty cells except `number` and `rail_minutes_to_imd` where defined blank; every `has_cta_rail = 1` row has minutes and no `has_cta_rail = 0` row does; every gazetteer target and every enclave member is a `name` in the CSV; both census-derived enclave lists reproduce from the CSV by their rule.
- Independent verification pass (a separate agent, read-only, own code): recomputed all 89 rows' shares, income and rent from the raw CMAP JSON (0 mismatches) and confirmed the hub CSV agrees for all 77; reran point-in-polygon with its own implementation (same 42 areas, same 20 outside stations); reproduced the router's direct-trip times for 5 origins from the raw stop times; parsed the brochures and the Wikipedia table itself; re-ran `build_tables.py` and got byte-identical outputs. It found the three defects fixed in the second build: the departure-event bug in the router, the missing `Lake View` key and three ambiguous alias strings in the gazetteer, and the overstated Wikipedia count in this file.

## Gaps and things I could not verify

- The gazetteer mapping is hand-written and has not been checked against a published neighborhood gazetteer; the landmark test above is the only external check.
- The main-station choice is a judgement. The per-station table removes the dependence: every station's time is there.
- Transfer penalties (60 s and 180 s) are assumptions, not CTA figures. The 32 areas whose journey needs a transfer were not reproduced independently; only the 14 direct-route main stations were.
- The Census Bureau API refused the request without an API key, so the Census Bureau's own tables were not read; Census Reporter pages (which serve the same ACS tables) were used instead. Census Reporter's profile page does not show median gross rent, so rent was not cross-checked for suburbs.
- The Census Reporter search API blocked the script; the suburbs' place GEOIDs came from the CMAP municipality table and agree with the four GEOIDs that TIGERweb returned for the geocoded stations.
- CMAP's community-area values are apportioned from tracts, so counts are floats and small areas carry large sampling error (Fuller Park: 2,345 people, four shares sum to 89.9 because 10 percent is non-Hispanic other).
- The CMAP release date (June 2026) comes from a search summary of cmap.illinois.gov, not from a page I read; the item's own modified timestamp (2026-06-15) is what the vintage is pinned to.

## Reproduce

```
unzip raw/cta_gtfs.zip -d raw/gtfs
python3 -I scripts/pip_stations.py raw/community_areas.geojson raw/cta_l_stops.csv raw/l_stations_by_community_area.csv
python3 -I scripts/filter_rail.py raw/gtfs
python3 -I scripts/rail_router.py raw/gtfs scripts/rail_times_2026-09-14_gtfs.json
python3 -I scripts/build_tables.py raw . scripts/rail_times_2026-09-14_gtfs.json
python3 -I scripts/xcheck_wiki.py raw/wikipedia_community_areas.html community_areas.csv raw/cmap_cds_2026_cca.json
```

`raw/gtfs/` holds the unzipped feed plus `rail_trips.txt` and `rail_stop_times.txt`, the rail-only subsets that `filter_rail.py` writes. The TIGERweb geocoding of the 20 suburban stations and the Census Reporter fetches were run inline; their outputs are in `raw/`.
